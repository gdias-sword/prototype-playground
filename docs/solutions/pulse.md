# Pulse

## Pulse Overview

Pulse is Sword Health's AI cardiometabolic care solution. It builds on what Move validated — that getting people to move more and move better meaningfully improves their health — by adding nutrition guidance, sleep optimization, behavioral health support, and connected health devices. Pulse delivers coordinated, always-on care for conditions including hypertension, prediabetes, type 2 diabetes, and obesity.

---

## How Is Pulse Different from Move?

Move focused on movement and exercise. Pulse keeps movement as the foundation but adds the additional care layers that cardiometabolic members need: nutrition, sleep, stress management, and behavioral support. It also introduces connected health devices (like blood pressure monitors) and proactive AI-driven engagement through Phoenix, our AI care agent, all via a 3-way chat between the clinician, member, and Phoenix.

---

## What Conditions Does Pulse Address?

At launch, Pulse will focus on hypertension, with expansion into prediabetes, type 2 diabetes, and obesity/weight management (including GLP-1 wraparound support). These conditions are deeply interconnected — 74% of people with diabetes also have hypertension — and Pulse addresses them as a coordinated whole, not in isolation. We will also eventually expand to support other cardiovascular conditions over our roadmap.

---

## How Does Pulse Work for the Member?

Members get a personalized care plan based on a whole-health evaluation. They receive connected devices (like a blood pressure monitor) and ongoing support through our three-way model: Phoenix (our AI care agent) provides 24/7 proactive engagement, while a clinician stays in the loop for oversight, clinical judgment, and high-value human moments.

---

## What Types of Clinicians Support Pulse Members?

The clinician type adapts to the member's primary need. For members needing movement support, a Physical Therapist. For more complex medical management, we are exploring introducing more clinical team members such as a Nurse Practitioner. One clinician per member, but the type varies based on what drives the most improvement.

---

## What Connected Devices Are Included?

Depending on the conditions, members may receive a wearable activity tracker (m-band), blood pressure cuff, scale, and smart pill bottle that connect to the Sword app. Future phases may include other monitoring devices such as glucose monitors based on member needs.

---

## Is Pulse a Wellness Program or a Medical Solution?

Pulse provides lifestyle recommendations and support to help people live well with and manage cardiometabolic conditions. We use connected devices and clinician oversight to ensure members receive high-quality, personalized guidance.

> **Note:** Per regulatory guidance, position as wellness/lifestyle. Avoid using "diagnose," "prevent," or "treat" in external-facing materials.

Pulse is purpose-built as a companion to GLP-1 therapy — helping members get the most out of their medication while reducing risks like muscle loss.

## What Does GLP-1 Support Look Like in Pulse? Is It Just Wraparound, or Will You Prescribe?

At launch (targeting June/early H2), Pulse includes wraparound GLP-1 support — lifestyle coaching, nutrition, movement, and medication adherence for members already on GLP-1s. ## How Would Pulse Integrate with Our PBM or Carrier for GLP-1 Step Therapy / Prior Authorization?

We're actively exploring PBM and carrier integrations. An initial lighter-weight option: clients provide a prescription claims file, and we target members on GLP-1s for enrollment. The longer-term vision is step therapy integration — requiring conservative intervention through Pulse before GLP-1 approval, or requiring Pulse enrollment as a condition of continued GLP-1 coverage. We have an existing partnership with Evernorth (Cigna) and Capital Rx/Judi Health that could be a starting point.

---

## How Does the Location/Calendar Integration Work? What if Someone Doesn't Use a Standard Calendar App?

Members opt in during onboarding to share calendar access (sees free/busy blocks only) and GPS. This is what allows contextual recommendations — like a desk stretch vs. a full workout, or walking the last stop of a commute. If a member doesn't opt in, the app still works — just without the contextual intelligence. We're exploring additional calendar integrations beyond the standard phone calendar sync.

---

## Will Exercise Recommendations Be Context-Aware?

Yes. Calendar sync tells us where a member likely is, and recommendations adapt accordingly — desk stretches at the office, full workouts at home. The system is contextual by design.

---

## What Happens When There's a Clinical Red Flag (e.g., Persistent Headaches with Hypertension)?

Phoenix escalates immediately to the human health specialist, who can then connect synchronously with the member and refer out to their PCP or another provider as needed. The AI is designed to recognize red flags and pull in clinical expertise — it doesn't try to handle safety-critical situations alone.

## Program Roadmap

### 1. Eligibility & Enrollment

- Member is invited to sign up for Pulse via outreach campaign (email, mailers, wellness benefit page)
- Member is identified as eligible through employer/health plan benefits file
- Member enrolls through the Sword app

In the future, we will consider doing targeted outreach with client-provided medical claims and Rx claims (e.g. people with cardiometabolic conditions or related medications). We will also consider offering biometric screenings (white labeled, most likely).

### 2. Device Selection & Shipping

Based on the member's primary condition, Sword ships the appropriate connected devices:

- **Hypertension** → blood pressure monitor
- **Prediabetes / weight management** → smart scale
- **All members** → can connect existing wearables (Apple Watch, Fitbit, Garmin, etc.) or use a Sword-provided wearable (m-band)

Sword-provided devices are included in the program cost — no charge to the member.

### 3. Onboarding

- Member connects devices, calendar, and wearables to the Sword app
- Member optionally consents to EMR data access via HIE onramps like Health Gorilla / Zus Health / Particle Health (medication list, medical history, lab values)
- Phoenix conducts an intake conversation to understand the member's daily routine: wake/sleep times, meal times, activity preferences, energy patterns, schedule preferences
- Member is assigned a health specialist (DPT or NP in the future, based on condition complexity)

### 4. Support Plan Creation

Based on the intake evaluation, device data, and any EMR information, a personalized wellness plan is created covering:

- **Movement programming** — type, duration, frequency matched to member's fitness level and schedule
- **Nutrition guidance** — DASH for hypertension, Mediterranean for general CV health, protein-focused for GLP-1 users
- **Sleep support** — wind-down routines, sleep hygiene education, guided meditations
- **Behavioral health** — stress management, breathwork, CBT-based exercises
- **Medication adherence reminders** — if member provides medication schedule

The health specialist reviews and approves the plan.

### 5. Daily Engagement (Ongoing)

This is where Pulse lives. Phoenix engages the member every day through the three-way chat:

- **Morning:** Check-in on sleep quality. Guided meditation or breathing exercise. Prompt for blood pressure reading. Medication reminder. Overview of the day's plan based on member's calendar and energy patterns.
- **Meal time:** Meal photo prompt before typical meal time. AI analyzes the photo and provides feedback aligned to the member's nutrition protocol (e.g., "sauces and dressings can have a lot of sodium — try half the dressing next time"). Tracks food trends over time.
- **During the day:** Sedentary alert if wearable shows extended sitting. Suggests a stretch, short walk, or movement break timed to the member's schedule ("15 minutes before your next call — try standing up").
- **Evening:** Prompts the recommended workout (bodyweight exercises, resistance bands, guided walk — matched to fitness level). Post-workout check-in. Dinner meal photo prompt and analysis. Evening BP reading if applicable. Wind-down routine (guided sleep meditation, body scan).

Phoenix learns the member's preferences over time through clinical memory — optimal engagement times, preferred communication style, which interventions the member completes vs. skips, which approaches drive results. Support adapts continuously.

We introduce these habits slowly over time, not all at once. For example: start with just using the scale or taking blood pressure regularly, then introduce logging meals with the camera on the phone in the app, then introduce movement. We aim for small amounts of movement, not intensity — 15-minute movement activities, slowly ramping up from there. The idea is for Phoenix to promote consistent habits over time, not intensity.

### 6. Health Specialist Oversight

- The health specialist has visibility into every Phoenix-member interaction
- Specialist reviews device data, engagement patterns, and Phoenix-flagged items on a regular cadence

Specialist intervenes directly in the three-way chat when:

- Phoenix detects unusual patterns (e.g., multiple days of elevated BP readings)
- Member reports symptoms (e.g., persistent headache, dizziness, lightheadedness)
- Member expresses frustration, disengagement, or emotional distress

### 7. Provider Coordination

With member consent, Pulse generates monthly summary reports for the member's existing physician:

- BP trends, weight trends, activity data
- Medication adherence data
- Progress toward wellness goals

Regular updates are sent via the HIE to the network for a member's providers to stay informed.

### 8. Outcome Tracking

Connected devices capture objective health data:

- Blood pressure readings (via connected BP monitor)
- Weight and body composition (via smart scale)
- Activity and sleep (via wearable) — daily

Health improvements are measured against national guidelines.
